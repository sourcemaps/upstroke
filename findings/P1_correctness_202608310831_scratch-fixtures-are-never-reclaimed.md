## Failure sequence

`src/rundir.rs`'s `scratch` calls `remove_dir_all` at **creation**, keyed by `{tag}-{pid}` — §16 records it in full. PR7 is the slice that pays for it: the suite grew from 1385 tests to **1644**, and the leak scales with the suite

## What the change that takes this up should do

Owner, as the ledger records it: project owner / whichever slice owns shared test infrastructure.

**Carried, unchanged in disposition from §16 and now with a second measurement.** The build box reached **19% of 58.5M inodes**; sweeping leaked fixture directories returned it to **12%** — on the order of **4.1 million inodes** that were leaked test fixtures, roughly a third of everything in use. `df -h` read 31% throughout. Held out of this slice for the reason §16 gives — the repair is a judgement call across 60+ call sites in shared test infrastructure, the PR5-round-7 shape — and mitigated out of tree by a sweeper with a 30-minute age floor so it cannot race a running suite. **PR7 raises the urgency rather than the difficulty**: parallel execution multiplies the fixture count per wall-clock hour. **2026-08-26: on Windows this stopped being a disk problem and became a correctness one.** The guest suite at `5e309a0` returned **16 failures** — fourteen in `engine::topology::emit::tests` and two in `settle::tests` — every one of them `assert!(bytes.is_empty(), "a fresh run has no prefix")` at `emit/tests.rs:324`. The same guest, minutes later, was **green at `040a100`** (1651 + 10, 0 failed), so it is not a regression in the diff. `emit/tests.rs`'s `run_paths` keys its scratch on `{tag}-{pid}-{n}` and **Windows recycles pids**: `%TEMP%` held **11,395** leaked `upstroke-*` directories, and grouping the `upstroke-emit-*` ones by their pid component gave six previous processes with 25-34 directories each. A run that draws a recycled pid finds its "fresh" fixture already populated and fails on the emptiness assertion. Sweeping `%TEMP%` to zero and re-running the same head is the control. **What this changes about the row**: the Linux symptom is inode exhaustion and is mitigated out of tree by a sweeper; the Windows symptom is a **fresh-run fixture that is not fresh**, it is indistinguishable from a real defect in the reviewed head, and no sweeper prevents it — the fix is that a fixture root includes something a recycled pid cannot supply, or removes its own directory at creation the way `rundir::scratch` does. Recorded here rather than repaired for the reason the row already gives: 60+ call sites in shared test infrastructure

**2026-09-17: a third measurement, an order of magnitude past the second, and two statements above that were false when checked.** Recorded from the build-box measurements the orchestrator made that night, not relayed from memory; the numbers are the ones it wrote down at the time.

**Scale.** The same box reached **100% of 58,466,304 inodes** (45,132 free). `open(O_CREAT)` failed for every process: background tasks were killed across the sessions on the box and every session moved its builds onto `/dev/shm` for the evening. `/tmp`'s top level held **1,731,834 entries**, every sampled one an `upstroke-*` fixture directory, with mtimes reaching back to **5 September**. Removing the entries older than 24 hours returned `/` to **20%** of inodes and took blocks from 93% to 66% used (61 GB to 290 GB free): roughly **45.7 million inodes** and some 220 GB were leaked fixtures, against the 4.1 million this row recorded. Twelve days of accumulation, and not the first exhaustion: §16 was itself filed when the box had already reached 100% once, at 1,639,765 entries. The 19% above was a reading taken between two exhaustions, not the leak's ceiling. `df -h` misled both times, as §16 says it would; only `df -i` shows it.

**The mitigation this row claimed did not exist.** "Mitigated out of tree by a sweeper with a 30-minute age floor" was checked on 2026-09-17 and was false: no systemd user or system timer, no cron entry, no script in `~/bin`. The only janitor on the box was stock `systemd-tmpfiles-clean.timer`, whose rule for `/tmp` is `D /tmp 1777 root root 30d` (`/usr/lib/tmpfiles.d/tmp.conf`), a **thirty-day** floor. That is why fixtures from 5 September were still present: nothing was ever going to remove them in time. Whether the sweeper this row described was never installed or did not survive is not known; either way the row's grade rested in part on it, and it was not there.

**A sweeper exists now, and it is still out of tree.** `~/bin/upstroke-tmp-sweep` on the build box, run by the systemd user timer `upstroke-tmp-sweep.timer` (15 minutes after boot, then hourly, `Persistent=true`), removes `/tmp` top-level `upstroke-*` entries older than **six hours** (`find /tmp -mindepth 1 -maxdepth 1 -xdev -name 'upstroke-*' -mmin +360 -exec rm -rf {} +`, at nice 19 and idle I/O) and appends one line per run to `~/upstroke-tmp-sweep.log`. Six hours rather than the thirty minutes this row mentioned, because a gate run takes about 2.5 hours and its fixtures stay live throughout; a thirty-minute floor races a running suite. Its first timed run, 2026-09-17T23:51Z, swept 80,503 entries that were between six and roughly twenty-four hours old. Three limits, so the next reader does not rediscover them. It is box infrastructure, deliberately not in this repository, and mitigates nothing on a CI runner, on the Windows guest, or on a developer's machine. It removes only what it can: six entries survive it today, two root-owned `upstroke-validate-*` directories from 3 September and four `upstroke-*.XXXXXX` roots from 4 September whose contents are unreadable. And it matches `upstroke-*` only, so the 125,570 `tactus-*` fixture directories, almost all from 22 and 23 August and left under the crate's previous name, are outside its pattern and still there. **The defect itself is unchanged**: the suite still creates a directory per fixture and never removes it on teardown, and the sweeper only decides how long a leaked one lives on one machine.

**Where the mechanism lives now.** The helper §16 quotes moved with the module split and is `src/rundir/tests.rs:41`, unchanged in shape (`upstroke-rundir-{tag}-{pid}`, `let _ = fs::remove_dir_all(&dir)` at creation); the `location` field follows it. The production scratch tree in `src/rundir/scratch_tree.rs` already does what the repair needs: a ULID-keyed root, an exclusive create that refuses an occupied root rather than pre-cleaning it, removal on drop, and a stderr report when reclaim fails. The shape the fix should take is therefore in the tree; what remains is the legacy helpers around it. At `3cfe883c` the pre-clean idiom, `let _ = fs::remove_dir_all(` or its `std::fs::` spelling, occurs **145 times in 25 files** under `src/` (`src/runner/host/tests.rs` 47, `src/agent/proc/tests.rs` 20, `src/runner/container/tests.rs` and `src/runner/container/census/tests.rs` 10 each, the rest fewer). Not every occurrence is a fixture pre-clean, so that count is the upper bound on the sites the repair has to judge, and it is above the "60+" this row said. Sibling rows on the same helpers: `PR64-CLEANUP-003-SCRATCH-PRECLEAN` (P1, the pre-clean deleting another process's root), `PREDICTABLE-SCRATCH-ROOTS-BEYOND-VALIDATE` and `PR245-WINGUEST-IMAGE-SHIPS-FIXTURE-RESIDUE`; the change that takes this up should close them together or say why not.

**Severity: the case each way, for the owner to rule.** This row is P2 on two grounds, and both failed on 2026-09-17: that the Linux symptom is a disk problem a sweeper was covering, when there was no sweeper and the box reached 100%; and that the correctness half is confined to Windows, when a box on which no process can create a file is a correctness failure of every suite running on it. The case **for P1**: the consequence was not a full disk but a machine on which `open(O_CREAT)` failed for everything, which killed background work in every session; it refills at the suite's rate (§16 measured a full run of the 1385-test suite leaking on the order of 2,400 directories, the suite is about 2,700 tests today, and the first timed sweep found 80,503 entries under a day old), so it recurs wherever nothing sweeps; and the Windows half is a required leg turning red on a defect that is not in the reviewed head, 16 failures indistinguishable from real ones; and a sibling defect of the same helper, `PR64-CLEANUP-003-SCRATCH-PRECLEAN`, already carries P1 on a demonstrated cross-process consequence. The case **against**: the defect lives in test infrastructure, the product's own scratch tree is correct, and no user of upstroke is affected; the Linux symptom is now mitigated on the one box where it has bitten, CI runners are ephemeral, and the Windows symptom has one observed instance with a known control (sweep `%TEMP%` and re-run); and a P1 commits a `max`-effort lane to a repair this row has twice judged too wide to do as a side effect of a slice. The reading of the session that wrote this amendment, for the owner to weigh and not to act on: both reasons the row gave for P2 are gone, what is left for P2 is only where the defect lives, and the Windows consequence is already the failure the row's category names, so the evidence favours P1.
